IETP Project

Our Chosen Topic

After a team brainstorming process, we selected this as our IETP project topic.

Current Stage: Topic Selected & Planning We have chosen our project topic and are now discussing how to approach it, dividing responsibilities, and starting our research phase.

"Design and Development of a Passive Thermal-Storage Solar Dryer
with Sensor Monitoring for Small-Scale Vegetable Drying"

Our team chose this topic because post-harvest vegetable loss is a real and common problem for small-scale farmers in Ethiopia. The idea is to build a solar-powered dryer that uses natural sunlight to dry vegetables, reducing spoilage without needing electricity. The dryer will also have temperature and humidity sensors so the drying process can be monitored. As the Software Engineering student on the team, I will be responsible for the sensor monitoring and software side of this system.

How We Got Here

🧠

Step 1 — Brainstorming

Each team member proposed ideas related to their discipline. We discussed multiple ideas including water purification, crop storage, and solar energy applications before narrowing down the list.

🗳️

Step 2 — Topic Selection

We evaluated each idea based on feasibility, available materials, budget (under 10,000 ETB), and how well it fits all six disciplines. The solar dryer idea was chosen because every discipline has a clear role in it.

📋

Step 3 — Planning

Now we are discussing how the project will work, what each discipline is responsible for, and what the timeline looks like. This portfolio documents my personal understanding and contribution as the project develops.

Why This Project Matters to Me

As a Software Engineering student, I find this project interesting because it combines software with a real physical system. Writing code that controls sensors and a fan in a real dryer is very different from classroom assignments — it has to actually work in the real world. The project also addresses a genuine community problem, which makes the work meaningful. I am still learning about the hardware side, but I am excited to figure out how to connect my software skills to this kind of system.

My Personal Contribution

What I Am Doing in This Project

This section describes my individual role as a Software Engineering student in the IETP team — what I plan to work on, why, and how it connects to the overall project.

I have two responsibilities in this IETP project, not one.

First, I am the Team Coordinator. That means I organize our meetings, keep track of what each person is doing, make sure the six disciplines are communicating with each other, and keep the project moving forward. This is a leadership and management role — it has nothing to do with code, but it is just as important.

Second, I am the Software Engineering member. That means I am responsible for the technical software side of the solar dryer — reading sensor data, displaying it on an LCD, and writing the logic that automatically controls the exhaust fan.

We are currently in the early stage — topic selected, roles just assigned, planning underway. Everything below reflects what I am doing now and what I plan to do as the project develops.

🎯

As Team Coordinator

I keep the team organized, run our meetings, resolve conflicts between disciplines, and make sure deliverables are submitted on time. Without coordination, six people from six disciplines would pull in six directions.

💻

As Software Engineer

I write the code that makes the dryer "smart" — reading temperature and humidity from sensors, displaying the data, and controlling the fan automatically based on that data.

🌉

The Bridge Between Both

Being both coordinator and SWE helps — I understand the full picture of the project, which makes it easier to define software requirements that match what the hardware team is building.
